# 2. How projects work

## Project types

| Type | When | Who | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weekly Build (WB)** | Most weeks | Individual (a few have a team part) | 50 |
| **Mini Project (MP1–MP5)** | Weeks 4, 8, 15, 19, 22 | Pairs (MP5 individual) | 100 |
| **Capstone (CAP-M1 to CAP-M4)** | Weeks 23–26 | Teams of 2–4 | 50, 50, 50, 100 |
| **Challenge (CH)** | Optional, from Week 8 | Individual | 50 |

- **Weekly Builds** are your weekly deliverable. They are reviewed and recorded, but they are not part of your final
  grade. Do them anyway: each one is practice for the weekly test and a piece of your portfolio.
- **Mini projects** are 15% of your final grade. The **capstone** is 25%: the Week 23 design review counts for 20% of
  that and the Week 26 final evaluation for 80%. The Week 24 and Week 25 checkpoints are for feedback only.
- **Challenges** are optional stretch work for when you are ahead.

To be eligible for a completion certificate you must submit all five mini projects and present your capstone.

## Reading a brief

Every project in your batch workspace has:
- a **brief**: what to build, split into MUST (needed for full marks) and STRETCH (extra, for practice);
- a **rubric**: the criteria and how many marks each one carries;
- an **open date** and a **due date**. Weekly Builds are normally due on Day 4 of the week at 23:59.

Start with the rubric. It tells you where the marks are.

## Submitting

1. Push your work to GitHub. Check that the repo opens when you are logged out, or that your tutor has access if it is
   private.
2. Open the project in your batch workspace and submit:
   - **GitHub repo URL** (required), in the form `https://github.com/your-username/repo-name`. Use the repo link
     itself, not a link to a folder or file.
   - **Live URL**, if the project is deployed.
   - **Notes**: what you built and anything incomplete. For team work, also list the features you own and your pull
     request links.
3. Your tutor reviews the repo as it is at review time. Do not rewrite history after submitting.

## After you submit

| Status | Meaning | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Submitted for Review | Waiting for review | Nothing yet (you can still push fixes and update the submission) |
| Under Tutor Review | Your tutor is reviewing it | Nothing yet |
| Changes Requested | Your tutor wants changes | Read the feedback, fix, push, and resubmit |
| Project Approved | Done | Read the feedback; it often contains points worth mentioning in interviews |

Approved projects earn XP towards your batch leaderboard.

## Team work

For team projects, every member submits the **same repo URL** in their own workspace and lists **their own** features
and pull requests in Notes. Marks can differ between team members. Your tutor checks the commit history and the project
board, and asks each of you about your own code.
